      The story's own timeline

      Years follow a traditional chronology built only from the biblical text (Floyd Nolen Jones, close to Archbishop Ussher's 1650s reckoning: Creation 4004 BC, Exodus 1491 BC). Historians and archaeologists date many events differently, and many early stories have no outside confirmation. Read these years as the narrative's internal clock.

          Quick answers about the Bible and this site

          How big is the Bible?
          66 books (39 Old Testament, 27 New Testament), 1,189 chapters and 31,102 verses. The Old Testament has 23,145 verses and the New Testament 7,957.
          What can I explore here?
          Every book drawn to its true length, a chapter reader that marks named people and places, a network of who appears with whom, a timeline of 501 events, and a map of about 1,200 places.
          Is this a religious site?
          No. It treats the Bible as a cultural and historical document and takes no religious position.
          Where does the data come from?
          Theographic Bible Metadata by Robert Rouse (CC BY-SA 4.0), the dataset behind viz.bible. Tags are automatic plus hand-checked, and the locations of many ancient places are uncertain.
          Which languages are available?
          16: English, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), French, Spanish, Arabic, German, Greek, Hebrew, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Swahili and Turkish.

          About this site

          The Bible is a great work of literature that has shaped much of human history. This site is here to make it more accessible, so you can read it, explore it, and see it in new ways.

          Data: Theographic Bible Metadata by Robert Rouse (CC BY-SA 4.0), the dataset behind viz.bible. Bible texts used in each language:

            How each language version was made: in every language, the interface, book names, eras and all 501 event titles were translated by hand, and the names of people and places come from that language’s Bible text. Hand-written summaries of people and places exist in Chinese (about 200 people and 64 places), French and Spanish (92 people and 16 places, plus machine translations marked as such); elsewhere a short summary is built from the data, with the English original one tap away. The Turkish text covers only part of the Old Testament, so missing verses show in English. Word counts in “Words and themes” use the English wording in every language.

            Data quirks to know: tags are automatic plus hand-checked, so a few are off. "Jacob (Israel)" ranks high partly because the nation "Israel" is tagged to him, and some nations (Moab, Midian) appear as people named for their ancestors. Locations of many ancient places are uncertain. This site presents the text as a cultural and historical document, without taking a religious position.
